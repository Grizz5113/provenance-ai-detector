Yes, there are passively managed funds that follow a "buy and hold" strategy. Passive funds, also known as index funds, aim to track the performance of a specific market index by holding a portfolio of securities that represents the index. The goal of these funds is to replicate the performance of the underlying index as closely as possible, rather than outperforming it through active management. As a result, they generally have lower expense ratios and fewer portfolio turnover than actively managed funds.Passively managed funds that follow a "buy and hold" strategy typically have a long-term investment horizon and do not frequently buy and sell securities in an effort to time the market. Instead, they seek to provide a low-cost and diversified way for investors to gain exposure to a particular market or asset class.It's worth noting that all investments carry some level of risk and it's important for investors to carefully consider their financial goals and risk tolerance before making any investment decisions.
