The IRA, or Irish Republican Army, was a militant organization that sought to end British rule in Northern Ireland and unite the island of Ireland. The organization used violent tactics, including bombings, to achieve its goals. The conflict in Northern Ireland, often referred to as "The Troubles," has its roots in the partition of Ireland in the 1920s, when the majority Protestant region of Ulster in the north was separated from the predominantly Catholic rest of the island and remained part of the United Kingdom. Many Irish Catholics, especially in Northern Ireland, opposed this arrangement and sought a united Ireland. The IRA was formed to fight for Irish independence and the reunification of Ireland. The Troubles were a period of sectarian violence and political conflict in Northern Ireland from the late 1960s to the late 1990s. The IRA and other militant groups, including Protestant paramilitary organizations, carried out bombings and other attacks in an effort to achieve their political objectives. The violence was often brutal and resulted in the deaths of thousands of people. The conflict was eventually resolved through a peace process that resulted in the Good Friday Agreement of 1998, which established a power-sharing government in Northern Ireland and set out a framework for relations between Northern Ireland and the rest of the United Kingdom. The IRA and other militant groups also decommissioned their weapons as part of the peace process.
