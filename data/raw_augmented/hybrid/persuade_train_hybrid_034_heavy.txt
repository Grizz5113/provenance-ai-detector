Reducing reliance on personal vehicles offers significant benefits. As Heidrun Walter observed, the absence of a car often leads to increased happiness, particularly within suburban environments. This stems from a greater ease of access to public transportation – a crucial advantage given the compact nature of many suburban communities. Without the need for a vehicle, individuals can avoid substantial expenses like gasoline and auto insurance, freeing up financial resources instead. Furthermore, suburban residents benefit from a shift towards localized amenities; stores are frequently within walking distance, reducing the dependence on automobiles.

Beyond these practical considerations, limiting car usage dramatically reduces environmental pollution. The elimination of exhaust emissions would mitigate the detrimental effects observed during both cold nights and warm days, effectively preventing smog formation. Replacing cars with alternatives, such as two-wheeled bicycles, can also foster a more focused urban landscape, allowing for innovative developments rather than prioritizing vehicular traffic.
