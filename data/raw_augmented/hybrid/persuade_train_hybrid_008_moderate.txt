People are arguing about keeping the Electoral College or changing to election by popular votes for the president of the United States. How do we know which one is better or safer? Well, what’s the solution? Keeping the Electoral College or transitioning to a system based on popular votes for the presidency?

In the text “In defense of the Electoral College: Five reasons to keep our despised method of choosing the president,” it states that “The Electoral College is widely regarded as an anachronism—a non-democratic method of selecting a president that ought to be overruled by declaring the candidate who receives the most popular votes the winner.” This highlights that the Electoral College appears outdated and doesn’t align with modern presidential selection processes. Furthermore, the text argues that the advocates for the Electoral College are correct in asserting that it is not a contemporary system; rather, electors ultimately elect the president, not the people themselves.

In the text “The indefensible Electoral College: why even the best-laid defenses of the system are wrong,” it states that “It’s official: The electoral College is unfair, outdated, and irrational.” This indicates a growing recognition of the inherent problems with the system, suggesting concerns about its fairness and potential disregard for public opinion in electing both presidents and senators.
