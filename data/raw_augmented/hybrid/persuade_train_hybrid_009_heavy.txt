To the Principal,

I believe a minimum grade average of B should be required for participation in extracurricular activities like sports and clubs, as the current system presents several inequities. It’s frequently the case that dedicated students struggle to maintain high academic standing despite their considerable effort, while others achieve higher grades with less dedication. This disparity creates an unfair situation where potential thrives alongside struggling performance.

Specifically, I've observed numerous students who consistently demonstrate a strong work ethic and commitment to their studies, yet are limited by grade point averages that prevent them from fully engaging in activities they genuinely desire. Conversely, some students achieve A grades seemingly effortlessly, without the same level of sustained effort. This imbalance highlights a fundamental problem: rewarding varying degrees of dedication with unequal access to opportunities.

The reality is that attaining a good grade requires significant commitment and perseverance; there are times when even the most diligent student faces setbacks and struggles to meet expectations. I’ve personally experienced this frustration, witnessing classmates who poured their energy into coursework only to receive a C, while others received As without comparable investment. 

Furthermore, participation in activities like sports or clubs offers invaluable benefits – promoting physical well-being, fostering social connections, and enriching the overall school experience. However, restricting access based solely on academic performance diminishes these advantages for students who possess both enthusiasm and a genuine desire to contribute, yet may not consistently achieve top grades. It’s important to recognize that participation should be accessible to those who demonstrate commitment and passion, regardless of their numerical grade point average.
