Dear Principal,

Changing the policy to require a grade B average for participation in sports or other activities seems unfair, as many students may struggle to maintain that level of achievement.

Sports and extracurricular activities often present significant time constraints for students, demanding considerable effort alongside homework and test preparation. If this policy were implemented, students would likely face intense pressure to cram, potentially negatively impacting their health and leading to feelings of anger or frustration.

Furthermore, altering the policy could limit participation and harm teams or groups. For example, if a key player fell just below a B average, it could detrimentally affect the team’s performance. No school desires consistent losses.

A grade B average would also represent a very stringent threshold. A score of 79.4, for instance, averages to a 79 – a mere one-point difference. It seems unduly harsh to exclude a student so close to meeting the requirement, particularly when a C average is technically passing and offers a more reasonable opportunity. Raising the standard to a B average would be excessively demanding for students already juggling numerous commitments.

Additionally, some students genuinely struggle with academic work and may only achieve barely passing grades. These individuals should not be excluded from activities simply because they process information differently.

Extra activities and sports are vital components of many students’ lives, promoting physical activity and social interaction. Denying participation based on a passing average (above 70) could be profoundly detrimental to these students, particularly for those relying on sports to secure college scholarships or gain valuable experience.

For some, sports provide an outlet for stress relief and relaxation – a welcome escape from academic pressures. Like a vacation, they allow individuals to focus solely on enjoyment and well-being.

Despite the importance of maintaining good grades, it’s equally crucial to foster engagement and promote physical activity. Changing the policy to require a grade B average to participate in sports or other activities is a misguided approach; such activities should be encouraged, not restricted.
