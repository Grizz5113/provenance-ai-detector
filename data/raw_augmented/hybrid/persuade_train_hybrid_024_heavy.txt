Dear Principal,

I am writing to express my serious concerns regarding the proposed change to require a grade B average for participation in extracurricular activities like sports and clubs. This policy feels fundamentally unfair, as many students simply cannot consistently achieve that level of academic performance.

Participating in these activities already presents a significant challenge for students, demanding considerable time and effort alongside their coursework and study commitments. Raising the bar to a B average would inevitably force students into a frantic cycle of cramming, leaving them with no time for essential rest, relaxation, or personal pursuits. This intense pressure could demonstrably harm their well-being, fostering feelings of anxiety and frustration – consequences that are entirely avoidable.

Furthermore, implementing such a stringent requirement risks excluding valuable members from teams and groups, potentially diminishing the strength of our athletic programs and other organizations. The loss of even one key player due to a marginal grade fluctuation could significantly impact team performance and jeopardize crucial games. Schools understandably strive for success, and this policy undermines that goal.

The proposed B average threshold is excessively demanding. A score of 79.4, for example, would be rounded up to a B, meaning a student with such a near-perfect record would still be denied participation. It seems unreasonable to penalize students who are only slightly below the required standard, particularly when a C grade represents a passing mark and offers a more realistic and equitable opportunity.

Moreover, it’s important to acknowledge that academic abilities vary greatly among students. Some individuals genuinely struggle with certain subjects and may consistently achieve only marginally passing grades. Excluding these students from activities solely based on their academic performance is both unjust and counterproductive. 

Extracurricular activities are vital for many students, providing opportunities for physical activity, social interaction, and personal growth. For some, sports represent a crucial pathway to college scholarships and higher education opportunities – essentially, a lifeline to a brighter future. The calming effect of engaging in these activities can also be incredibly beneficial, offering a much-needed respite from academic pressures and allowing students to de-stress and enjoy themselves.

In conclusion, I firmly believe that changing the participation criteria to a B average is a detrimental policy. Instead of imposing such restrictive standards, we should actively encourage student involvement in sports and other activities, recognizing their profound importance for both physical and mental well-being.
