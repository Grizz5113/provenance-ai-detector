The electoral college, established by the Founding Fathers to allow citizens to vote for the President and Vice President, incorporates a process of counting these votes by Congress. As described in “the Office of the Federal Register,” concerns have been raised regarding the electoral college’s viability due to potential voter confusion and misdirected votes; however, such errors are now significantly less frequent. Ultimately, the candidate who receives the most votes becomes the next President and Vice President.

To begin with, establishing the electoral college represents a beneficial system for selecting a candidate desired by the people. It provides an avenue for every citizen of the United States to express their opinion and vote for the individual they believe will best lead the country. This system ensures that each person’s voice is heard in the selection of the President.

Secondly, the electoral college maintains the organization surrounding the counting of votes and the declaration of the candidate who receives the most popular votes – the winner. When a voter casts their ballot, they are essentially voting for a slate of electors chosen by Congress; therefore, citizens decide which individual they wish to be president, prioritizing their opinion and ensuring it shapes the outcome. While the electoral college has experienced occasional errors, advancements in modern technology have minimized these issues and improved its overall efficiency.

In conclusion, many people today appreciate the Founding Fathers’ creation of the electoral college because it remains a superior system. Every individual within the United States can vote for the candidate they prefer, solidifying the importance of their voice in the selection of the President and contributing to the well-being of the nation – as exemplified by our current president's effective leadership.
