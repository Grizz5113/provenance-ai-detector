Dear Principal,

I strongly suggest allowing students to have their cell phones available during lunch and free time. We should definitely turn them off during class time, as this is crucial for ensuring we receive our education. During class, if some students are distracted by their phones, they will not fully engage with the lesson or pay attention to what the teachers are saying. I propose that upon entering the classroom, students hand their phones over to the teacher or demonstrate that they have turned them off. This would allow the teacher to confirm student attentiveness. Having cell phones in class is disruptive and unproductive. What if a test is approaching, and the class is reviewing it, while a student remains disengaged? That student could fail simply because of their phone’s distraction. The only appropriate times for students to have their phones are during free time and lunch periods. There is no learning occurring then, so why not allow its use? While there may be minimal harm in having phones out during these times, any defiance or abuse of the phone rule should result in consequences. Students would receive three warnings: 1) a verbal warning, 2) temporary removal from class until the end of the period, and 3) requiring a parent to retrieve the device. I believe it is beneficial that we are permitted to have phones out during these times; however, if these rules are consistently violated, students should be restricted from having phones at our school entirely.

Sincerely,

Student
