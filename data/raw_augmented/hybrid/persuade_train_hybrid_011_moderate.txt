My position is that driverless cars are problematic due to my lack of trust in their reliability. I have several reasons for this concern. First, the potential for accidents is a significant worry. Driverless car systems could malfunction and cause vehicles to veer off course, resulting in collisions with objects or other parties – including police cars, buses, or even pedestrians. 

Second, a proliferation of driverless cars would exacerbate existing traffic congestion. With so many vehicles relying on automated driving, the sheer volume of cars on the road would increase dramatically. This could overwhelm junkyards, leading to increased production and potentially reducing available space for housing or agricultural land.

Finally, widespread adoption of driverless cars raises serious environmental concerns. If a large number of people purchased these vehicles, air pollution levels would likely rise significantly, negatively impacting public health. Furthermore, any issues with alternative fuel sources – such as a malfunction in the gas conversion process – could lead to catastrophic consequences, including vehicle explosions or overwhelming pollution levels, ultimately eroding public confidence and halting further adoption.
