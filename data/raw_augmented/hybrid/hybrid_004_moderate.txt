Summer is a student’s time to pursue their interests and passions. When teachers assign summer projects designed by themselves, students can feel as though they are still in school, which can negatively affect their break. Summer projects should be student-designed because it is their time, they understand what a design would best suit them, and some students prefer to engage with the real world rather than completing traditional assignments.

It’s their time—a period for students to explore activities outside of the classroom and pursue interests they genuinely enjoy. Some students gravitate towards sports like soccer, football, or basketball, while others find enjoyment in playing video games during the summer break. For some, summer is a chance to set ambitious goals, such as reading one hundred books, visiting ten new countries, or learning one hundred new words. Summer break holds special significance for students.

Students can design projects that align with their individual needs and preferences. Each student is unique, and their friends are often the best people to understand them. Instead of a teacher designing the project, a student’s friend could take on this role, choosing a subject that the student finds both interesting and possesses knowledge in. Teachers can offer suggestions for summer projects, but students retain the autonomy to choose what they would like to do.

Some students thrive when engaging with experiences rather than solely relying on reading or writing. Instead of spending twenty pages on an assignment, some students prefer to immerse themselves in a subject—perhaps through diving, traveling, or playing sports. Experiencing something can be vastly different from simply reading about it, and friends should recognize these preferences. Many students seek summer jobs to earn money and gain valuable experience for their future careers.
