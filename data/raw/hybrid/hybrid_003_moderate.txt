The author describes how a new technology called the Facial Action Coding System (FACS) enables computers to identify human emotions by allowing us to detect exactly how others are feeling without being explicitly told. A new software has been developed to cultivate knowledge of these emotions. Although current applications can calculate math homework, the technology’s origins lie in analyzing works like the Mona Lisa.

Dr. Paul Ekman is the creator of FACS; he utilized a computer to create 3-D models of faces expressing basic emotions. First, the author describes how FACS enables computers to identify human emotions through observation. This highlights the technology's ongoing development and its potential to expand our understanding of this new era. The Mona Lisa was used in this discovery: “She’s 83 percent happy, 9 percent disgusted, 6 percent fearful, and 2 percent angry.” This demonstrates that even when viewing a famous painting like Leonardo da Vinci’s Mona Lisa, we cannot discern all the subtle emotions represented.

Second, the author describes how FACS enables computers to identify human emotions simply by looking at a person's normal facial expression. This illustrates how the technology has evolved over time, with new discoveries continually emerging. Dr. Paul Ekman is the creator of FACS. He classified six basic emotions – happiness, surprise, anger, disgust, fear, and sadness. This discovery allows us to identify these six fundamental emotions based on Dr. Ekman’s research.

Third, the author describes how FACS enables computers to identify human emotions by presenting a single image of an individual's expression. This technology demonstrates that anyone can gain knowledge about this topic without necessarily being an expert. To achieve this, computers are used to create 3-D models of faces expressing basic emotions. The text states: “The process begins when the computer constructs a 3-D computer model of the face; all 44 muscles in the model must move like human muscles.” This indicates that each of the six emotions is associated with specific, characteristic movements of facial muscles.

In conclusion, the Facial Action Coding System is useful for understanding the characteristic movement of the facial muscles and their connection to human emotion.
