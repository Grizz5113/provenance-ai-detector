Driverless cars represent the future, and they have the potential to be truly great. With these vehicles, we can reduce our concerns about safety – both our own and others’.

Safety is paramount, and these cars are inherently safer. As noted in an article, “In reality, Google has had cars that could drive independently under specific conditions since 2009. Their cars have driven more than half a million miles without a crash” (Paragraph 2). These driverless cars have been proven safe. Many drivers do not have wrecks, so those people are safe drivers. However, there are also individuals with one or multiple wrecks, and these accidents may not be their fault; nonetheless, they still occurred. The Google-tested driverless cars have yet to experience a crash. The prospect of driverless cars eliminating the risk of loss of life in car accidents is truly amazing.

Another positive aspect of driverless cars is their adherence to traffic rules. These vehicles do not speed, run red lights, or fail to stop at stopsigns; they consistently follow regulations. Some leading causes of wrecks – speeding, running red lights in a rush, and disregarding stop signs – are mitigated by the fact that driverless cars operate within established guidelines. Furthermore, factors like texting and drinking, major contributors to accidents, are eliminated because driverless cars cannot be distracted or intoxicated. Driverless cars will not be pulled over for being intoxicated or swerving.

Some may argue, “These really aren’t driverless cars; you still have to drive them.” This is accurate as of now. However, individuals should be comfortable with occasionally taking control. The text states, “Google cars aren't truly driverless; they still alert the driver to take over when pulling in and out of driveways or dealing with complicated traffic issues, such as navigating through roadwork or accidents” (Paragraph 2). And it also says, "...none of the cars developed so far are completely driverless. They can steer, accelerate, and brake themselves, but all are designed to notify the driver when the road ahead requires human skills, such as navigating through work zones and around accidents" (Paragraph 7). So they are correct when they say they are not truly driverless. But for now that is the technology we have we should accept it, and who would not want to feel as if they were in control during situations such as a wreck, or a work zone. And if we all ended up having driverless cares we would not have to worry about us having to take over the car through a wreck zone because these cars do not wreck.

A potential downside is that future generations may never learn how to drive themselves. Driving is often a favorite activity for people as they reach driving age, and this opportunity will be lost with driverless cars. While younger individuals might not initially miss the experience of learning to drive, they would be missing out on a privilege enjoyed by countless generations worldwide. Conversely, parents would undoubtedly appreciate the peace of mind knowing their children are safe from wrecks.

The future is approaching, and everyone needs to prepare! Driverless cars represent a positive development for our planet, and we should all be excited about this advancement. Safety has always been a universal desire, and driverless cars offer that assurance.
