There are several ways that statistics can be manipulated, either intentionally or unintentionally. One way is by cherry-picking, which means selecting only certain data that supports your argument and ignoring data that does not. This can make it seem like there is a relationship between two things when there may not be one. Another way is by using misleading graphs or charts. For example, you could use a graph with a Y-axis that starts at a number other than zero, which can make it seem like there is a larger difference between the data than there really is. A third way is by using statistics that are out of context. For example, if you say "The number of car accidents has increased by 50% in the past year," it might sound alarming, but it's important to know if the number of cars on the road has also increased by a similar amount. It's also important to be careful about making causal connections based on statistical data. Just because two things are correlated, it doesn't necessarily mean that one caused the other.
