Peer-to-peer (P2P) lending is a type of financing that allows individuals to borrow and lend money directly to one another, without the involvement of traditional financial institutions like banks. In P2P lending, small notes refer to smaller loans that are typically made to individual borrowers, while large notes refer to larger loans that may be made to businesses or organizations.There are a few key differences between small notes and large notes in P2P lending:Borrower risk: Smaller loans, or small notes, may be considered less risky than larger loans because they are typically made to individual borrowers who are less likely to default on their loans. On the other hand, large notes may be considered more risky because they are often made to businesses or organizations, which may be more vulnerable to financial challenges.Loan terms: The terms of small notes and large notes may also differ. For example, small notes may have shorter repayment periods and lower interest rates, while large notes may have longer repayment periods and higher interest rates.Investment opportunities: Investors may have different preferences when it comes to investing in small notes versus large notes. Some investors may prefer the stability and lower risk of small notes, while others may be willing to take on more risk in exchange for the potential for higher returns from large notes.It's worth noting that P2P lending is a relatively new and rapidly evolving industry, and the specific terms and conditions of small notes and large notes may vary depending on the platform and the borrower's creditworthiness. It's always a good idea to thoroughly research and understand the terms of any investment before committing to it.
