Instant hand warmers work by using a chemical reaction called exothermic reaction. Exothermic reactions release energy in the form of heat.\n\nInside the hand warmer, there are two packets. One packet contains a solid form of iron and the other packet contains water and a special chemical called sodium acetate. When you shake the hand warmer, the solid iron and the water and chemical mixture mix together. This causes a chemical reaction that releases heat. The heat warms up the hand warmer and your hands when you hold it.\n\nThe chemical reaction will continue as long as there is iron and the water and chemical mixture present. When the hand warmer cools down, you can reactivate it by clicking a small metal disc inside the warmer. This will start the chemical reaction again and the hand warmer will heat up again.\n\nDoes that make sense?
