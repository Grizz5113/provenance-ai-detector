A credit union is a type of financial institution that is owned and controlled by its members, rather than by shareholders. Credit unions offer many of the same financial products as banks, including loans and savings accounts.When you take out a loan from a credit union, you will typically be required to pay interest on the loan. The interest is a fee that you pay to the credit union for borrowing money. The rate of interest will depend on the terms of the loan and your creditworthiness.In addition to offering loans, credit unions also often offer savings accounts to their members. These accounts pay dividends, which are essentially a share of the credit union's profits. Dividends are typically paid out to members on a regular basis, such as monthly or quarterly. The rate of dividends that you earn on your savings account will depend on the credit union's policies and the amount of money that you have saved.In general, credit unions tend to offer more favorable terms and lower fees than traditional banks. They may also be more willing to work with you if you have credit challenges or are in a financially difficult situation.
