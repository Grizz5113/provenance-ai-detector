When you sell a non-dividend stock, you are simply selling the ownership stake in the company represented by that stock. You are not entitled to receive any dividends from the company as a result of the sale. On the other hand, if you sell a dividend-paying stock, you may be entitled to receive a dividend payment from the company, depending on the timing of the sale and the company's dividend payment schedule. If you sell the stock before the ex-dividend date, which is the date on which the stock begins trading without the dividend, you will not be entitled to receive the dividend. If you sell the stock on or after the ex-dividend date, you may be entitled to receive the dividend, depending on the company's payment schedule and the specific terms of the dividend.
