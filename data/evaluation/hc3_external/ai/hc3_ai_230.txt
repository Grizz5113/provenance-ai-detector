In this system, "cousin" refers to a relative who shares a common ancestor with you, but is not a direct ancestor or descendant. So for example, your parents' siblings' children (your first cousins) would be your first cousins.

The term "removed" refers to the number of generations between you and your cousin. So if you and your cousin are in the same generation (meaning you are the same age), you would be "first cousins." If your cousin is one generation younger or older than you, you would be "first cousins, once removed." If your cousin is two generations younger or older than you, you would be "first cousins, twice removed," and so on.

Here's an example to help illustrate this:

Imagine that you are the grandchild of two siblings (your grandparents). Your grandparents' siblings (your great-aunts and great-uncles) are your first cousins. If one of your first cousins has a child, that child would be your first cousin, once removed. If that child has a child, that child would be your first cousin, twice removed.
