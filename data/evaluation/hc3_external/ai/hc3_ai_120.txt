Shares, also known as stocks or securities, represent ownership in a company. When you buy shares in a company, you are essentially becoming a shareholder, which entitles you to a share of the profits and voting rights at shareholder meetings.Physically, shares do not have a tangible form. Instead, they are represented by a certificate or a record in an electronic database. In most cases, shareholders do not receive a physical certificate, but rather, their ownership is recorded electronically in a system maintained by a stock exchange or a central securities depository.Shares can be bought and sold through a stock exchange, such as the New York Stock Exchange or the NASDAQ. When you want to buy or sell shares, you can do so through a brokerage account or through a financial advisor. The price of a share is determined by supply and demand, and it can fluctuate based on a variety of factors, such as the company's performance and overall market conditions.Shares can be used in a number of ways. For example, shareholders may choose to hold onto their shares as an investment, in the hope that the value of the shares will increase over time. Alternatively, shareholders may decide to sell their shares if they believe the value of the shares is likely to decline or if they need the money for other purposes. Some shareholders also receive dividends, which are payments made to shareholders out of the company's profits. Dividends can be paid in cash or in the form of additional shares.
