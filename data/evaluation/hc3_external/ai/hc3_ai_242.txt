School buses are designed to be safe and provide a secure transportation option for students. One of the ways they are made safe is by having high, padded seats that are positioned close together, which helps to protect passengers in the event of a collision. These seats are also designed to absorb some of the impact of a collision, which helps to further protect passengers.

In addition to the high, padded seats, school buses also have other safety features such as flashing lights and stop signs to alert other drivers to the presence of the bus, as well as reinforced steel frames and rollover protection to help keep passengers safe in the event of a collision.

As for seat belts, many school buses do not have them because they are not required by federal law. However, some states do require seat belts on school buses, and some school districts have chosen to install seat belts on their buses as an additional safety measure.

If a collision were to occur on a school bus and someone was injured, it is possible that they could potentially sue the school district if they believe that the district was negligent or failed to take appropriate safety measures. However, it is important to note that each case is different and would need to be evaluated on its own merits.
