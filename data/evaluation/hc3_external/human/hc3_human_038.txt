So you can break beer into two families : ales and lagers . They use two different types of yeast , ales ferment at the top and lagers on the bottom . ( the yeast hangs out there while it 's alive ) also , lagers endure the process of ... Lagering , which means cold storage , they hang out at 35ish degrees for a few months . Now , you see a lot more variety in ales ( especially in microbrews ) because they generally take a shorter time to ferment ( no lagering process ) . Now the American/ English family of ales goes sort of like this from light to dark . Blonde / extra pale , pale , amber / red , brown . You also have India pale ales , which is generally an aggressively hopped version of a pale ale . Darker than brown ales are porters then stouts , which generally have chocolate , or roasty flavors . Most of the modifiers used in names are actually fairly simple once you figure out what they mean . Hmm , what else ... Wheat beers . You have Witbiers ( ex : blue moon ) that are Belgian style wheat beers . Hefeweizens are german wheat . APWA , or American pale wheat ale is what American style wheats should be called , but they are often labeled as hefeweizens because that sounds pretty .
