There are basically two ways of doing salvage like this : a ) refloating the boat b ) cutting it up in place . To refloat the boat depends on how badly damaged it is , you 'd have to patch up big holes , reinforce the superstructure and then add something to make it float again . Often this is large inflatable air - bags , or occasionally they do this thing where they basically pump ping - pong balls into the ship which floats it up , even if it still has holes leaking water . The benefit of this is that the ship is much more of a re - usable state . Depending on the damage the ship once re - floated may be able to be repaired , refitted and returned to service . However the given the Costa Concordia 's position on the rocks it might be impossible to refloat it without breaking it apart more , and given that it is sitting on a ledge the last thing they want is to shift it and then have it slide off the ledge and into deep water . The other option would be to cut it up in place , that 'd be less ideal obviously because you 're then reducing the value of the ship to its raw materials , mostly steel for scrap . The ship will be cut apart manually by workers and divers , then the scrap metal loaded onto another ship . That scrap will then usually end up at a foundry being melted down to be cast into new metal products .
