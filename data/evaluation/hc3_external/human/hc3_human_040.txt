Several reasons . Aside from the fact the demand is n't there because nothing is distributed in 4 K , 3D or not , they 'd actually be pretty hard to produce for a number of reasons . Examine for example the fact that a 4 K display has 3840x2160 pixels . That 's over 7.5 million individual little squares to issue colour directions to every refresh rate , which is n't particularly easy at the best of times . Now consider that not every one of those pixels is created equal . Some will only be able to refresh 30 times a second ( 30Hz ) , others might manage ( 75Hz ) , and some might even get up to 120Hz+ without encountering problems . Some might not even be able to operate at 30Hz with a full colour set , and some will just be completely dead from the start . The chances of having a full 30 " panel with no defective pixels all capable of refreshing 120 times a second is pretty low , so yields are low , so supply is very limited . In order to create a decent stereoscopic 3D experience , the whole panel needs to be able to operate at 120Hz in order to provide a 60Hz image to each eye . A 30/30 split from 60Hz is possible but considered severely sub - optimal and is not something I can see people paying a lot of money for .
