It 's not that statistics are easily manipulated . It 's more that statistics without the proper context can be misleading ( intentionally or unintentionally ) . Supposedly , the number of kids diagnosed with autism increased by 300+% in the 1990 's . A series of new vaccines were introduced in the 1990 's that contain small amounts of mercury . Both statements are true , but are unrelated . The statistic that autism cases increased by 300+% is completely unrelated to the introduction of new vaccines , but because some people tried to make the association and reported the 2 statements in the same article , the statistic became very misleading . Btw , numerous studies have shown that vaccines are safe and are not contributing to the increase in the number of cases of autism . It 's not to say that there was n't an increase in the number of cases . It 's only to say that vaccines were not the cause .
