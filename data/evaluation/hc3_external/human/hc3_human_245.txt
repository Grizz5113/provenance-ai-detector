A lot of people do n't know this , but a checking account is technically called a DDA ( Demand Deposit Account ) because the bank has to produce your funds whenever you might demand it . Technically , a savings account is not a DDA and so the bank can take some amount of time to deliver your withdrawal requests . Also with a savings account , the bank can limit how many withdrawals you can make , such as 3 a month , or 6 a month . A DDA is a type of " transactional account , " which means it is not meant to earn but to make transacting business easy , and they traditionally do n't pay interest . A savings account is meant to be kept on deposit for longer periods of time and these typically do earn interest . The reality is that in the modern banking world the lines between these types of accounts are often blurred . Many checking accounts earn interest and many savings accounts allow you to write checks or come with debit cards . But the distinction is still made , often for regulatory purposes . For example , deposits in savings accounts do n't count towards a bank 's reserve requirement , because the bank is n't necessarily required to deliver up the money to a customer on demand .
