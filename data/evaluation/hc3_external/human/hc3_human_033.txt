Pretty good question - it has to do with how the energy of the impact is transferred to the tile . When something falls and hits the ground , a force of sufficient energy to stop the object is applied at the point of contact . In the case of a hard weight , which likely lands on an edge or a corner , this force is applied to a tiny area which experiences a large stress ( basically a pressure , represented by force divided by area ) . In the case of a person , while the magnitude of the force may be 5 , 10 , or 20X higher , it is spread over a MUCH larger area and so the stress on the floor is actually lower . In addition to this , a person 's foot and body compress and absorb the energy of the impact over a distance , as opposed to a hard weight which does not yield at all . This lowers the amount of energy transferred to the floor , and it 's the reason why cars are designed to crumple in a crash .
