I started enjoying science when I was in school because experiments made ordinary lessons feel less predictable. I still remember trying to build a small electric circuit with my classmates and arguing about why the bulb would not turn on.

Technology has significantly enhanced the educational experience by providing students with access to interactive resources, personalized learning opportunities, and immediate feedback. These tools can help learners understand difficult concepts while allowing teachers to identify areas where additional support is required. Nevertheless, technology should complement rather than replace meaningful interaction between teachers and students.

What stayed with me was not whether our experiment worked perfectly. It was the fact that we kept changing one thing at a time until we understood what was wrong. That habit has stayed with me whenever I have to solve a difficult problem.
