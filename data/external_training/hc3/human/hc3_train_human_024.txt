Others have posted far better explanations in the past , but here 's a shot at it : The operators were running a turbogenerator run - down test where the energy stored in the steam turbines would be used to power the main circulating pumps until the backup diesel generators could come on ( ~1 - 2 minutes ) . During preparations for the test , the reactor power output fell drastically due to xenon buildup ( a fission product that absorbs neutrons and acts like a virtual control rod ) . To counteract this , the operators withdrew a huge number of control rods . As the power level came back up , more boiling occurred in the channels due to the pumps spooling down . This further increased power because of poor reactor design ( " positive void reactivity coefficient " ) . The operators realized this , and hit the AZ-5 ( SCRAM ) buttom . Unfortunately , the control rods displace water for the first few cm of their travel , which increased reactivity more and caused an even more drastic power excursion . This ruptured the control rod guides and the reactor spontaneously disassembled in the next few seconds .
