Secured convertible notes (SCNs) are debt instruments that can be converted into equity at a later date, typically under specific conditions. These conditions may include a predetermined conversion price, a passage of time, or the occurrence of a specific event. SCNs are typically issued by startups and other early-stage companies as a way to raise capital while giving investors the option to convert their investment into equity at a later date.Here is a general outline of how the conversion of SCNs typically works:An investor provides capital to a company in exchange for SCNs. The SCNs will typically have a fixed term, during which the investor is entitled to receive periodic interest payments.The SCNs will also have specific conversion terms, which outline the conditions under which the investor can convert their SCNs into equity. These conversion terms may include a conversion price, which is the price at which the SCNs can be converted into equity, and a conversion period, during which the investor can choose to convert their SCNs into equity.If the specified conversion conditions are met, the investor can choose to convert their SCNs into equity by surrendering the SCNs and receiving an equivalent number of shares in the company.If the investor does not choose to convert their SCNs into equity, the SCNs will typically mature at the end of their fixed term, at which point the investor will receive the principal amount of their investment plus any accrued interest.It is important to note that the terms of SCNs can vary significantly from one issuer to another, and investors should carefully review the terms of any SCN before making an investment.
