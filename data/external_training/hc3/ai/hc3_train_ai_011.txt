AIDS is caused by a virus called HIV, which stands for human immunodeficiency virus. HIV attacks a person's immune system, which is the body's natural defense against illness and infection. When a person's immune system is weakened, they become more vulnerable to other infections and diseases. 
When a person has HIV, they may not have any symptoms for a long time, but the virus is still active in their body and damaging their immune system. Eventually, the HIV virus can progress to AIDS, which stands for acquired immune deficiency syndrome. When a person has AIDS, their immune system is severely damaged and they become much more likely to get other infections and diseases. These infections and diseases can be very serious, and they can be difficult to treat because the person's immune system is so weak. 
When a person with AIDS gets a serious infection or disease, their body may not have the strength to fight it off. If the infection or disease is not treated in time, it can cause the person to become very sick and eventually die. So, in short, a person can die from AIDS if their immune system becomes so damaged that they are unable to fight off serious infections and diseases.
