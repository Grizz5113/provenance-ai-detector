Dear Principal,

I am writing to advocate for allowing students to bring cell phones to school. The reality is that many of us drive to school, necessitating a way to coordinate pick-ups with family members. Furthermore, students frequently participate in after-school activities and require reliable communication regarding changes or urgent needs. 

I personally experienced the necessity of having a mobile phone when I joined the junior varsity basketball team in seventh grade. Without it, I wouldn’t have been able to notify my coach about unexpected practice schedules or changes – as happened several times when games were unexpectedly moved from home to away locations. During one particular instance, a last-minute text from my mother informed me that she needed to pick up an item and that I was now responsible for walking home; had I not had my phone, I would have missed the game entirely, leaving her waiting at the school while I was still on the bus. 

I believe that allowing cell phones can improve communication between students, parents, and school staff. For example, a text message could quickly inform parents of unexpected changes to after-school plans – such as a sudden shift from a home game to an away game – preventing unnecessary trips to the school. I recall receiving a notification informing me that my mother needed to pick me up because she had to run an errand; without my phone, I would have been stranded and delayed. It’s crucial for students, particularly those involved in extracurricular activities, to maintain open communication regarding their whereabouts and schedules after school hours. 

While I acknowledge concerns about potential misuse during class time – such as cell phone use distracting from learning or instances of theft – I believe a balanced approach is possible. If a student uses their phone inappropriately, the device should be temporarily confiscated, with its return scheduled for the end of the day. Ultimately, providing students with access to cell phones offers a practical and efficient means of communication that enhances safety and coordination, particularly when managing after-school commitments.
