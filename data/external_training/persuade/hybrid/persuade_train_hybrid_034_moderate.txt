Limiting car usage offers several advantages. For example, individuals often experience stress when reliant on a vehicle, as Heidrun Walter noted in her story—a matter of smart planning. In suburban life, not owning a car is more compact and accessible to public transportation. Consequently, people would avoid unnecessary expenses like gas and car insurance, freeing up funds for other uses. Furthermore, suburban residents benefit from a shift towards walkable communities where stores are within easy reach.

Additionally, limiting car usage significantly reduces pollution. Without vehicle emissions, the air would be cleaner during both cold nights and warm days, eliminating smog. Rather than relying on cars – which can be stressful and distracting – individuals could utilize alternatives such as a two-seated bike. Cities could then focus on developing new initiatives instead of prioritizing automobile use.
