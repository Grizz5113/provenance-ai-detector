Dear Principal,

I believe we should be allowed to bring cell phones to school because many students drive to school and need someone to pick them up. Additionally, students often need to contact their parents if something changes or if they require assistance after school hours. When issues arise – whether it’s during school, while walking home, or with extracurricular activities like sports – having a phone readily available is incredibly helpful.

I received my first cell phone in seventh grade when I made the junior varsity basketball team after school. Without it, I wouldn't have been able to communicate if practice was canceled or if there were any changes. During my time on the team, we sometimes had home games, and my parents wanted to attend. However, these plans would frequently change at the last minute, and without cell phones, they could show up at school unexpectedly, causing disruption. I understand the principal’s concern about students getting into less trouble; however, it would be beneficial if students were able to communicate effectively.

It would also be advantageous for us to bring cell phones to school because parents need to be informed of any changes in plans. For example, one time my mom sent me a text message letting me know I was needed as a walker because she had to pick up something after school and we had a game scheduled. If I hadn’t had a phone, I would have taken the bus like I normally did that morning and missed the game entirely; my mom would have been waiting at the school, and I would have been home late. It is important for students to have access to phones because parents and children need to be aware of what's happening after school and where they need to be. 

For this reason, I believe it’s a good idea for students to have cell phones at school, allowing them to check messages during lunch or when bus/walker schedules are announced. However, if a student is using their phone inappropriately during class, the teacher should take it away and return it at the end of the day.
