To Whom It May Concern,

I firmly believe that elementary school students should be encouraged to collect their own trash. I am writing to advocate for this simple act, arguing that it cultivates a sense of responsibility and empathy – qualities that are invaluable. Frankly, refusing to participate in such an effort seems inconsiderate. If someone were to ask me if I would assist in picking up litter, I would undoubtedly respond with “yes,” recognizing the positive impact it has on maintaining clean environments. It’s a small gesture that contributes significantly to keeping our spaces tidy.

However, I question the implementation of mandatory community service for students. While contributing to the upkeep of one's neighborhood is undeniably beneficial, it’s important to acknowledge potential conflicts with personal commitments. Many students have existing obligations – extracurricular activities, family responsibilities, or simply the need for downtime – and forcing them into a structured volunteer role may prove counterproductive. In my opinion, only a small fraction of students would genuinely embrace such an undertaking; perhaps no more than five or six out of a group of twenty-five would consistently participate. The vast majority might be unwilling to even explore whether they find it fulfilling.

Furthermore, the consequences of littering extend far beyond mere aesthetics. We’ve all witnessed the tragic deaths of animals caused by carelessly discarded trash. Imagine, for instance, a deer desperately searching for sustenance, only to stumble upon a discarded fast-food wrapper. Driven by hunger, it might consume the packaging, leading to illness or, tragically, death. My sister, my mother, and I are actively involved in the ASPCA’s Animal Protection Program, and we share a profound aversion to littering. We believe everyone should consider this issue seriously.
