Exploring Venus

I wonder why Venus is called the “Evening Star.” I think Venus was sometimes referred to as the evening star because it’s a bright planet and shines like a star; it’s also the second planet from our sun. And Venus is often referred to as Earth's twin because it is the closest planet to Earth. If I were an astronaut, I would love to explore Venus – it would be fascinating to walk on its surface.

Most people should visit Venus simply to see how it looks and what it’s like. I think NASA has a crucial role in exploring planets. As our jet airplanes travel at higher altitudes to fly over many storms and go into space to explore other plants, we can learn so much.

The National Aeronautics and Space Administration (NASA) has done a great job when they have gone into space and discovered planets. It’s very cool to go on other planets; Venus is sometimes closer to Mars. The Earth rotates itself, and people don't always notice it.

The sun is the hottest planet in the solar system – it’s over 100 degrees. Long ago, Venus was probably covered largely with oceans and could have supported various forms of life just like Earth.

Today, Venus still has some features that are analogous to those on Earth, but it remains one of the most interesting planets in our solar system.
