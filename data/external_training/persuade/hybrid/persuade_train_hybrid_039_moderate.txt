In my opinion, online schooling would not be ideal for a child. Children in traditional public or private schools interact with peers daily, which fosters natural social development. Students taking online classes at home may find themselves feeling awkward when interacting with others outside of video conferencing, as they lack regular face-to-face interactions.

Parents might believe that homeschooling offers advantages, but it often hinders a child’s social skills and can lead to a misunderstanding of the material being taught. Research has demonstrated that children learn more effectively in collaborative environments, particularly when observing their teachers and classmates. Students in public or private schools develop knowledge alongside friendships, benefit from experiences like field trips, recess, and—most importantly—field day.

Another significant concern regarding online classes is the excessive screen time and limited human interaction. I believe this can be detrimental to a child’s development; children simply need the presence of other kids to thrive at times, someone who can match their energy levels consistently. Homeschooled students may miss out on what I consider to be the most exciting aspects of childhood.

In conclusion, children in public or private schools tend to be more social and outgoing than those in homeschooling environments; they also typically learn more effectively as a group and have a richer overall learning experience.
