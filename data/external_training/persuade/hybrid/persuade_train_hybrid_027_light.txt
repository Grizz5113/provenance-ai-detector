What if students were able to design their own projects for the summer? Would that be more effective in helping a student learn, rather than a teacher designing it? Most students would love to have the chance to design their own projects for summer. They’d be excited about the chance to make the project manageable, but who could blame them – nobody likes doing work over the summer.

Teachers should design summer projects instead of students. Students are often prone to trying to do the least amount of work by any means possible. There are some students who'd try to challenge themselves so they could actually learn, but the majority of students would make the projects easy. Students just don’t like completing hard tasks, especially when they are on vacation.

By a teacher designing it, they will know what to include in the projects. Students don’t always know what they are expected to learn for the upcoming year; that's the teachers’ job. Teachers have a list of units to complete each school year. They’d probably put those units on the projects, which would easily prepare the students.

In conclusion, teachers should design the projects for summer to allow students to continue learning over the summer and also to have the students ready for the upcoming school year by already showing them what they will learn.
