What if students were able to design their own projects for the summer? Would that be a more effective way to help a student learn, rather than a teacher designing it? Most students would welcome the chance to design their own projects for the summer, and while they’d likely want to make the project manageable, who could blame them – nobody really enjoys doing work during vacation.

Teachers should design summer projects instead of students. Students often prioritize minimizing effort, and while some might seek challenging opportunities to truly learn, the majority would understandably opt for simpler tasks, particularly when on break. Students simply don’t enjoy completing difficult assignments, especially when they are anticipating a period of leisure.

When a teacher designs the project, they possess crucial knowledge about what students are expected to learn for the upcoming year. Teachers have detailed lists of units to complete throughout the school year, and they would likely incorporate these directly into the summer projects, effectively preparing students in advance. 

In conclusion, teachers should design the summer projects to ensure students continue learning over the summer and are well-prepared for the upcoming school year by being shown what they will learn.
