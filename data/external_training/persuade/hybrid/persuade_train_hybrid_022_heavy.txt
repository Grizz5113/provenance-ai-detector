As a Seagoing Cowboy, I’ve had the extraordinary privilege of traversing continents and witnessing diverse cultures firsthand. My journeys have taken me to Greece, across Europe, and even to China – experiences entirely unique to this profession. Anyone considering a career as a Seagoing Cowboy should seriously consider it; it offers access to worlds otherwise unattainable.

The life of a Seagoing Cowboy is defined by camaraderie and purposeful work. I made nine distinct voyages by 1947, the highest number among my colleagues, and each one was filled with rewarding activity. Beyond caring for horses, cows, and mules – vital tasks that formed the core of our operations – we engaged in a wide range of activities designed to both entertain and prepare us for demanding careers. We played volleyball and table tennis, honed our skills through fencing and boxing, immersed ourselves in literature, and practiced the art of whittling. These diverse pursuits fostered a profound awareness of global needs and the richness of human experience. I particularly recall the vibrant conversations we had about the various countries we visited, recognizing the distinct challenges each faced. The return trips from unloading livestock were especially joyous, transforming even lengthy month-long voyages to places like China into remarkably swift experiences. During my time in Greece, a truly unforgettable moment occurred when I was able to visit the Acropolis – a sight that remains deeply etched in my memory. Furthermore, arriving in Greece just before my eighteenth birthday allowed me to enlist in the military, an opportunity I embraced with enthusiasm. Remarkably, our arrival on August 14th coincided with the end of the Pacific War, a momentous occasion that underscored the significance of our service.

In conclusion, I wholeheartedly recommend pursuing a career as a Seagoing Cowboy. It represents a singular and enriching experience, one that combines adventure with invaluable learning, ultimately shaping you into a more informed and capable individual.
